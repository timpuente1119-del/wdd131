General Steps for the Experiment

  • 1. Dissolution Phase (Leaching)

    • Equipment Needed: Heated magnetic stirrer, reaction glassware (beaker or round-bottom flask), thermometer, and an analytical scale.

    • Chemicals Used: Unsaturated fatty acids (derived from sunflower/cooking oil), 30% aqueous hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), and sodium dodecyl sulphate (SDS) surfactant.

    • Process: E-waste (such as silver-plated keyboard connectors or circuit boards) is mixed into the fatty acid and H₂O₂ solution. The surfactant is added to accelerate the reaction. The solution is slightly heated and stirred continuously.

    • Temperature: Room temperature up to 60°C.

    • Time Required: A few hours (the dissolution rate maxes out at approximately 1.62 mol/m²⋅h).

  • 2. Separation Phase (Precipitation) • Equipment Needed: Glass funnel, filter paper or a vacuum filtration system, and separation glassware. • Chemicals Used: Ethyl acetate solvent. • Process: Ethyl acetate is poured into the liquid mixture. This causes the newly formed silver carboxylates to drop out of the solution into a solid form while leaving the unreacted fatty acids behind to be recycled. The solid silver precipitate is then isolated via filtration. • Time Required: 15 to 30 minutes for rapid precipitation and physical separation.
  • 3. Recovery Phase (Photochemical Reduction) • Equipment Needed: Dual 30 W Compact Fluorescent Light (CFL) lamps (emitting 400–650 nm wavelengths) and a transparent holding vial. • Chemicals Used: Ethanol (alcohol) solvent. • Process: The isolated solid silver carboxylate is suspended in an ethanol medium. The suspension is exposed directly to the specified light wavelengths. This light-driven reduction breaks down the carboxylate compound, leaving behind pure elemental silver powder. • Time Required: Several hours to overnight depending on the volume of the sample
